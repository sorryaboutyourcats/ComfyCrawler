        ⚙️ Options
        Medium 3DMaze Labyrinth: 66 corridors with branching paths, lanterns and a distant Exit.
        Generation Settings
        Optimized is a middle tier: tiling textures, player frames and enemy sprites at 384×384, the switch & lantern cutouts and HUD portraits at 192×192. Meaningfully faster than High Quality without Reduced's coarseness. Reduced halves the resolution of everything (a 512×512 texture becomes 256×256) for the fastest, lightest dungeon. High Quality is the full-quality default.
        Music adds two 30-second Stable Audio 3 tracks (explore/battle) on top of the sound effects, which takes noticeably longer to generate. Skip disables all generated audio for the fastest dungeon.
        Display Settings
        12240
        The ceiling on how often the dungeon is redrawn. Your monitor is still the real limit – this only ever asks for fewer frames than it offers. Combat keeps its own 60-per-second clock, so lowering this never slows the fight down.

        🎮 What this is

        You fill in four blanks on the main menu – a style, a hero, a weapon, a foe – and a stack of models running on this machine builds the rest: the walls, ceiling and floor, the hero and their swing, three different foes, the story that scrolls past while you wait, the names, the footsteps, the music, and (if you switch it on) a short film of the final blow.

        Then you walk it. Every dungeon you finish making is kept in dungeon_sessions/ and can be started again from 📜 History for nothing, because the expensive part already happened. That window reads either way: as rows, or as tiles – each dungeon drawn as its own hero standing in it.

        🎮 What this is

        A play-only copy of ComfyCrawler. Every dungeon in the list started as four blanks – a style, a hero, a weapon, a foe – and a stack of models running on sorryaboutyourcats’ own PC built the rest: the walls, ceiling and floor, the hero and their swing, three different foes, the story that scrolls past before you start, the names, the footsteps, the music, and on some of them a short film of the final blow.

        All of that was made ahead of time, so here there is nothing to wait for: pick one and walk it. The pictures and sounds are fixed, but the maze is not – it is laid out fresh every time you press Start, at whichever difficulty is picked, so the same dungeon plays differently twice.

        Want one with your own four blanks in it? 🛠️ Make your own runs, under the list, has both ways to get one.

        🛠️ How it was made

        Written by sorryaboutyourcats together with Claude, in Claude Code on Opus 5. One Python server and one JavaScript file, no build step and no framework beyond Tailwind off a CDN.

        sorryaboutyourcats decided what it should be and played every build; Claude wrote the code and measured what came back.

        🧠 What does the drawing

        None of these run in this page. They ran once per dungeon, on one NVIDIA RTX 3090 through ComfyUI, and what they made is what you are playing.

        Krea 2 Turbo — walls, ceilings and floors, the hero’s nine poses, and all three foes. Eight steps at cfg 1.0, which means there is no negative prompt at all: every word is something being asked for. krea2_turbo_fp8_scaled · qwen_image_vae
        Qwen3-VL 4B — Krea 2’s own text encoder, doing double duty as the writer: the intro crawl, the hero and boss names, and the three foe species. It also looks at a finished sprite to decide whether the flying one gets wings or thrusters. qwen3vl_4b_fp8_scaled
        FLUX.1 Kontext dev — the only model here that edits a picture instead of drawing a new one: the four HUD portrait faces, the flyer’s thrusters, and the boss’s scorch and embers. flux1-dev-kontext_fp8_scaled · t5xxl_fp8_e4m3fn · clip_l · ae
        BiRefNet — cuts every sprite off its background, which is what lets a hero drawn on white stand in a corridor. birefnet.safetensors, via KJNodes
        Stable Audio 3 Small SFX — eleven one-shots per dungeon, written from the same typed styles as the art: the footstep matches your floor, the swing matches your weapon, and both death cries match who they belong to. stable_audio_3_small_sfx · t5gemma_b_b_ul2
        Stable Audio 3 Small (the checkpoint from before the one-shot finetune) — the thirty-second exploration and battle loops, crossfaded at the seam so you cannot hear where they join. stable_audio_3_small_sfx_base
        MiniMax H3 (reference-to-video-and-audio) — the optional ending cutscene: eight seconds at 512×384 with its own soundtrack, filmed from this run’s own hero, boss and textures as reference pictures and its battle music as a reference track. minimax_h3_ref2va_pruned_int8_convrot · qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_nvfp4_awq · video + audio VAEs
        Piper — the voice reading the intro crawl. The one model that runs inside the Python server rather than in ComfyUI. en_GB-alan-medium · en_US-kristin-medium
        🔌 How it is plumbed

        A Python 3.10 standard-library HTTP server on 127.0.0.1:5555 serves the page and owns every run. Each model above is reached through ComfyUI on 127.0.0.1:8188: graphs are posted to its /prompt, finished images and audio are collected from /history, and the loading bar is fed by its WebSocket. SageAttention is switched on for the cutscene when the node is installed.

        No cloud API, no account, no key, no MCP server. The only thing this page talks to is your own machine, and nothing you type into it leaves.

        🔌 How this page works

        It is the same page and the same game code as the full version, with generating switched off. There is no server behind it: just static files, a list of the dungeons, and one saved bundle per dungeon holding its pictures, sounds, music, story and names. The maze, the foes’ moves and the fights all run in your browser.

        No account and no tracking. Your options, stars and which bosses you have beaten are kept in this browser only, and go nowhere else.

        💾 What is on this disk

        Every file the pipeline loads, and whether this machine’s ComfyUI can see it. Click one to open the folder it belongs in. Anything missing can be downloaded from the setup panel on the main menu.

        🛠️ Make your own runs

        Every dungeon here started as four blanks – a style, a character, a weapon and an enemy – and a stack of models drew, wrote, named, scored and voiced the rest. Two ways to get one with your own four in it:

        💻 Run it yourself

        ComfyCrawler is free and open source. It needs ComfyUI and a GPU with plenty of VRAM (developed on an NVIDIA RTX 3090; other GPUs untested), plus about 50GB of models – 95GB with the sound, music and ending cutscene too – and the setup screen downloads whichever of those are missing for you. A dungeon then takes a few minutes to build, and is yours to keep and replay.

        🏰 ComfyCrawler on GitHub github.com/sorryaboutyourcats/ComfyCrawler
        ✉️ Or send me the four prompts

        No graphics card, or no interest in installing anything? Message me your four and I'll run them here. Anything goes for the style – a place, a mood, a single word, a name in “quotes”. Write them out in one go, like this:

        CHARACTER – a tired flight attendant
